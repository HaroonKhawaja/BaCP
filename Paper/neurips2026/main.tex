\begin{abstract}
Gradual magnitude pruning drives a network to extreme sparsity by removing
weights on a schedule while the surviving weights continue to train against a
cross-entropy objective. We asked whether replacing that objective with a
contrastive one --- which keeps the sparse student's representations aligned
with those of the dense model it was initialised from --- produces better
sparse models under an otherwise identical budget. The module decomposition we
used (pruning-, fine-tuning- and snapshot-contrastive terms) is CAP's
\citep{xu2022cap}, which was reported on pre-trained language models only; our
contribution was to carry it to vision backbones, to test whether it is
agnostic to the pruning criterion, and to push it into a sparsity regime CAP
did not report. We trained ResNet-34/50 and VGG-11/19 on CIFAR-10 under three
global pruning criteria --- magnitude, SNIP-it and WANDA --- at sparsities of
95\%, 97\%, 99\% and 99.9\%, against an iterative-pruning baseline that shared
the identical schedule, criterion, epoch budget and fine-tuning stage and
differed \emph{only} in the training objective. In an objective ablation on
ResNet-50 under magnitude pruning, the contrastive objective improved top-1
accuracy over that matched baseline by \result{abl.resnet50.delta.magnitude.0.95}
points at 95\% sparsity and \result{abl.resnet50.delta.magnitude.0.99} points at
99\%, against a run-to-run noise floor of \result{cifar10.noise.floor.lo}--%
\result{cifar10.noise.floor.hi} points; a variant with a frozen tied projection
head, closer to CAP's published form, reached only
\result{resnet50.ablcontrol.magnitude.0.95} at 95\%. Across the full matched
sweep the mean improvement was \result{summary.delta.mean} points and the
largest single gain was \result{summary.delta.max} points, and the effect was
not confined to a single criterion: it held under SNIP-it and WANDA as well as
magnitude, at \result{summary.delta.mean.bycriterion} points respectively.
We report absolute accuracies below the published sparse-training literature
because our budget is 50+25 epochs at batch 512 rather than 160 epochs at batch
128, and we make no state-of-the-art claim; the controlled claim is the
objective delta under a matched budget.
\end{abstract}
