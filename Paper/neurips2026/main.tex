\begin{abstract}
Gradual pruning drives a network to extreme sparsity while the surviving
weights keep training against a cross-entropy objective. We asked whether
replacing that objective with a contrastive one --- one that keeps the sparse
student's representations aligned with those of the dense model it was
initialised from --- yields better sparse models under an otherwise identical
budget. The module decomposition we used (pruning-, fine-tuning- and
snapshot-contrastive terms) is CAP's \citep{xu2022cap}, which was reported on
pre-trained language models only; our contribution was to carry it to vision
backbones, to test whether it is agnostic to the pruning criterion, and to push
it into a sparsity regime CAP did not report. We trained ResNet-34/50 and
VGG-11/19 on CIFAR-10 under three global pruning criteria --- magnitude,
SNIP-it and WANDA --- at sparsities from 95\% to 99.9\%, against an
iterative-pruning baseline that shared the identical schedule, criterion, epoch
budget and fine-tuning stage, differing in the training objective under an
otherwise matched protocol. In an objective ablation on ResNet-50 under magnitude pruning, the
contrastive objective improved top-1 accuracy over that matched baseline by
\result{abl.resnet50.delta.magnitude.0.95} points at 95\% sparsity and
\result{abl.resnet50.delta.magnitude.0.99} points at 99\%, against a
run-to-run noise floor of \result{cifar10.noise.floor.lo}--%
\result{cifar10.noise.floor.hi} points. Absolute accuracies sit below the
published sparse-training literature because our budget is 50+25 epochs at
batch 512 rather than 160 epochs at batch 128; we make no state-of-the-art
claim, and the controlled claim is the objective delta under a matched budget.
\end{abstract}
