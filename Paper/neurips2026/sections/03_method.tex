\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Pipeline}

Every cell in this study followed the same four stages. A backbone was
initialised from ImageNet-pretrained weights \citep{deng2009imagenet}; it was
fine-tuned dense on the target task to produce the reference model; it was then
pruned gradually to the target sparsity while being trained under one of two
objectives; and the resulting masked network was fine-tuned to convergence with
the mask held fixed. This is the prune--retrain paradigm of
\citet{han2015learning} with the gradual schedule of \citet{zhu2018prune}, and
the contrastive supervision of \citet{xu2022cap} inserted at the third stage.
Initialisation was enforced programmatically: the training entry point refused to
start when the weights it loaded were not the requested provenance, because a
soft-failing checkpoint load is otherwise indistinguishable from a run that
trained from scratch.

The two arms differ at the third stage and nowhere else. The
\textbf{I.P.} (iterative pruning) arm trained under cross-entropy. The
\textbf{BaCP} arm trained under the four-term contrastive objective below.
Sections~\ref{sec:method:prune} and \ref{sec:experiments} describe the schedule,
criteria, budget and fine-tune, all of which were shared.

\subsection{Objective}

BaCP's training objective was the $\lambda$-weighted sum of three contrastive
terms and a cross-entropy term,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:objective}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PrC}}
                + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FiC}}
                + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SnC}}
                + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}},
\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{4}\lambda_i = 1 ,
\end{equation}
%
which is the objective of \citet{xu2022cap} with the $\lambda$ indices permuted.
We set $\lambda_i = 0.25$ for all $i$, so the four terms entered at equal weight
and no weight was tuned. Constraining the weights to the simplex is deliberate:
it fixes the total scale of the objective, so that changing the balance between
terms cannot also change the effective learning rate.

Let $f_\theta$ denote the sparse student, $g$ a projection head, and
$z = \mathrm{normalize}(g(f_\theta(x))) \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$, so that
$z_i^{\top} z_j$ is a cosine similarity. Let $\hat f$ denote a frozen teacher and
$\hat z$ its correspondingly projected, normalised embedding; teacher forward
passes ran under \texttt{no\_grad}, so $\hat z$ carried no gradient and each
contrastive term acted as a distillation objective rather than a joint embedding
objective. With $\tau > 0$ a temperature, the contrastive functional for an
anchor $i$ with positive set $P(i)$ drawn from $N$ candidates is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:functional}
\mathcal{L}_i \;=\; -\frac{1}{|P(i)|}\sum_{j \in P(i)}
\log \frac{\exp\!\left(z_i^{\top}\hat z_j / \tau\right)}
          {\sum_{k=1}^{N}\exp\!\left(z_i^{\top}\hat z_k / \tau\right)} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} \mathcal{L}_i ,
\end{equation}
%
Equation~1 of \citet{xu2022cap}, averaged over the anchor set
$\mathcal{A} = \{\, i : |P(i)| > 0 \,\}$. Anchors with an empty positive set were
excluded from the mean rather than divided by a clamped denominator; \emph{we
define} this convention, since dividing by a clamped $1$ would contribute a
spurious zero to the average.

Each module instantiates Eq.~\eqref{eq:functional} twice against the same
teacher, over the same candidate set and therefore the same denominator: once
\emph{self-supervised}, with $P(i) = \{\hat z_i\}$, the teacher's view of the same
image; and once \emph{supervised}, with $P(i) = \{\hat z_j : y_j = y_i\}$, every
candidate sharing the anchor's label \citep{khosla2020supcon}. The module loss is
the sum of the two, $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{module}} =
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{self}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}$. The three modules
differ only in which frozen network supplies the candidates:

\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PrC}}$ contrasts against the \textbf{pretrained}
  model $\phi_{\mathrm{pre}}$ --- the general-purpose representation, before any
  task adaptation.
\item $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FiC}}$ contrasts against the \textbf{fine-tuned}
  dense model $\phi_{\mathrm{fine}}$ --- the task-specific representation the
  sparse network is trying not to lose.
\item $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SnC}}$ contrasts against the \textbf{snapshots}
  $\phi_{r'}$, $r' < r$, saved at earlier points of the same pruning run --- the
  student's own recent past, which anchors the trajectory against drift as the
  mask tightens.
\end{itemize}

This three-teacher decomposition is Table~1 of \citet{xu2022cap}. With $K$
snapshots accumulated, we \emph{averaged} over them rather than summing,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:snc}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SnC}} \;=\; \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{module}}\!\left(\phi_{r_k}\right),
\end{equation}
%
so that the term's scale is independent of $K$. Under a plain sum the SnC term
grows linearly as snapshots accumulate, which silently rescales $\lambda_3$
relative to the other three weights over the course of a single run; the average
removes that coupling and is what we ran.

\paragraph{The instantiation we ran.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:functional} admits more than one realisation, and the
difference is not cosmetic. \citet{xu2022cap} evaluate it over a rectangular
$B \times N$ matrix of student anchors against teacher candidates. The
instantiation we ran --- referred to below as the \emph{legacy} objective --- forms
$Z = \mathrm{cat}[z_s, z_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{2B \times D}$ and computes the
supervised and self-supervised terms as SupCon \citep{khosla2020supcon} and
NT-Xent \citep{chen2020simclr} over the square $2B \times 2B$ matrix $ZZ^{\top}$,
in the SimCLR all-pairs convention. The square matrix therefore carries
student--student and teacher--teacher blocks that CAP's rectangular form does not,
and it makes teacher rows anchors as well. We implemented both forms and chose
between them by measurement rather than by inheritance; the controlled comparison
is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} and the legacy form was the one that won
at every measured point, which is why it is the objective this paper studies.
Throughout, $\tau = 0.15$.

\subsection{Pruning}
\label{sec:method:prune}

\paragraph{Schedule.}
Sparsity was raised along the cubic schedule of \citet{zhu2018prune},
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cubic}
s_t \;=\; s_f\left(1 - \left(1 - \frac{t}{T}\right)^{\!3}\right),
\end{equation}
%
their Eq.~1 with initial sparsity $s_i = 0$ and start step $t_0 = 0$, where $s_f$
is the target sparsity, $t$ the optimizer step and $T$ the end of the ramp. The
ramp occupied the \textbf{first 80\%} of the pruning phase's optimizer steps; for
the remaining \textbf{20\%} the mask was frozen at $s_f$ and training continued,
which is the ramp-then-recover proportion of \citet{gale2019state}. Splitting the
budget this way matters more at extreme sparsity than at moderate: the last
increments of the ramp remove the largest fraction of what is still alive, and a
schedule that reaches $s_f$ at the final step leaves no steps in which to recover
from them.

\paragraph{Mask updates.}
Mask recomputation was driven by optimizer steps, not epochs: the mask was
recomputed every $\Delta T = 100$ steps and re-applied after every step in
between. At batch 512 on 45{,}000 training images, 100 steps is approximately one
epoch. We used $100$ rather than the exact per-epoch count of $87$ because
\citet{zhu2018prune} tested update intervals between 100 and 1000 steps and
report the effect as negligible across that range; $87$ would sit below anything
they measured. Driving updates from steps rather than epochs also keeps the
schedule well-defined when the number of batches per epoch changes.

\paragraph{Criteria.}
The three criteria differ only in the scoring function applied to each surviving
weight. All three were then thresholded \emph{globally}, at the single value that
achieves the current $s_t$ over the whole prunable scope, so that the criterion is
the only variable that changes between columns of our results table:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:criteria}
S^{\mathrm{mag}}_{ij} = \left| w_{ij} \right| ,
\qquad
S^{\mathrm{snip}}_{ij} = \left| w_{ij} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial w_{ij}} \right| ,
\qquad
S^{\mathrm{wanda}}_{ij} = \left| w_{ij} \right| \cdot \left\lVert X_j \right\rVert_2 .
\end{equation}

$S^{\mathrm{mag}}$ is magnitude pruning \citep{han2015learning}, and under
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cubic} it is gradual magnitude pruning in the sense of
\citet{zhu2018prune}. $S^{\mathrm{snip}}$ is the SNIP saliency
\citep{lee2019snip}, used unnormalised and applied iteratively through training
after \citet{verdenius2020snipit} rather than once at initialisation.
$S^{\mathrm{wanda}}$ is the WANDA metric \citep{sun2024wanda}, with
$\lVert X_j \rVert_2$ the norm of the $j$-th input feature accumulated over the
current batch. Two conventions here are ours rather than WANDA's and we mark them
as such. \emph{We define} the global comparison group: WANDA's published form
ranks within each output row, and its authors report that this grouping choice
does not transfer from language models to image classifiers, so we adopt the
global threshold used by the other two criteria in order to isolate the score.
\emph{We define} the 4-D convention: WANDA's reference implementation covers
\texttt{Linear} layers only, and we fold a convolution kernel into the input
dimension so that a weight of shape $(c_{\mathrm{out}}, c_{\mathrm{in}}, k_h, k_w)$
is scored against unfolded input columns, producing the same matrix WANDA would
score for a linear layer.

\paragraph{What was pruned, and what was not.}
The prunable scope excluded 1-D tensors (biases and normalisation parameters) and
frozen tensors. The \textbf{classifier head was pruned}, following the vision
pruning line we compare against: \citet{han2015learning} prune the output fully
connected layer, SNIP thresholds globally over all weights, and RigL and GraNet
mask the classifier by default \citep{evci2020rigl,liu2021granet}. VGG's
backbone MLP, which holds the large majority of those models' parameters, was in
scope. The \textbf{projection head $g$ was never pruned}, under either arm, and
was \textbf{discarded before the final fine-tune}, so it appears in no reported
model and contributes to no reported parameter count. The scope was byte-for-byte
identical between I.P.\ and BaCP within every cell of every table.

Reported sparsity is the fraction of \emph{prunable} weights that are masked. We
state the denominator explicitly because at 99.9\% the choice of denominator moves
the reported number materially, and because the prunable scope --- not the full
parameter count --- is what our threshold acts on.
