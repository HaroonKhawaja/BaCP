\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Unstructured pruning removes weights from a trained network and retrains what
remains \citep{han2015learning}. Up to moderate sparsity this is close to free:
a cubic gradual schedule \citep{zhu2018prune,gale2019state} recovers essentially
dense accuracy on CIFAR-scale classifiers at 90--95\% of weights removed. Past
that point the picture changes. As the surviving budget shrinks toward a few
tenths of a percent, retraining stops being repair and becomes re-derivation:
the network must rebuild a representation from a support too small to hold the
one it had. Methods within a fraction of a point at 90\% separate by tens of
points at extreme sparsity \citep{li2025east}.

Cross-entropy supplies at most $\log_2 C$ bits per example and constrains only
the decisions, not the representation those decisions are read from --- and at
extreme sparsity the representation is exactly what the mask destroyed. A
contrastive term against a frozen dense teacher instead supplies a
$D$-dimensional target direction per example rather than a scalar; the appendix
develops the mechanism in full.

That objective is not new. \citet{xu2022cap} introduced Contrastive Pruning
(CAP), which supervises a model during gradual pruning with three contrastive
terms against three frozen teachers --- pretrained (PrC), task-fine-tuned
(FiC), and the sparse snapshots saved so far (SnC) --- combined with
cross-entropy into a four-term weighted sum, on BERT-family encoders to 97\%
sparsity. \textbf{The PrC/FiC/SnC decomposition, and the four-term objective
that combines them with cross-entropy, are CAP's, not ours.} We state it here,
not in a footnote, because it bounds what follows;
Section~\ref{sec:method} sets out where our instantiation differs.

We contribute a controlled study of that objective --- \emph{Backbone
Contrastive Pruning} (BaCP) --- in three settings CAP did not examine: (i) an
extension from language models to convolutional vision backbones, ResNet-34/50
and VGG-11/19; (ii) criterion-agnosticism --- magnitude \citep{han2015learning},
SNIP-it \citep{lee2019snip,verdenius2020snipit} and WANDA \citep{sun2024wanda}
were run under one global threshold, so the criterion was the only variable
between blocks; and (iii) the extreme-sparsity regime, run to 99.9\% where CAP
reported 97\%. Every BaCP run was matched against an iterative pruning (I.P.)
baseline identical in schedule, criterion, epoch budget, fine-tune and data ---
everything but the objective --- so $\Delta$ is attributable to the objective
alone, and $\Delta$, not any absolute accuracy, is the unit of evidence.

\paragraph{Related work.} The prune--retrain template \citep{han2015learning},
its cubic ramp \citep{zhu2018prune} and the ramp-then-hold shape audited at
scale \citep{gale2019state} are adopted here; the three criteria above
span magnitude, gradient and activation saliency. At-init and lottery-ticket
criteria \citep{frankle2019lottery,wang2020grasp,tanaka2020synflow} meet layer
collapse as their extreme-regime failure mode. Dynamic sparse training
\citep{mocanu2018set,evci2020rigl,liu2021granet,li2025east} is context, not a
baseline: this paper studies a static mask, and an objective-level and a
support-level intervention are separable. The loss forms composed here are
SimCLR's and SupCon's \citep{chen2020simclr,khosla2020supcon}, with CRD
\citep{tian2020crd} the antecedent for a contrastive term where logit matching
\citep{hinton2015distilling} would go. \citet{blalock2020state} document how
rarely pruning papers are mutually comparable --- the direct motivation for the
matched-budget design. The appendix treats each line in full.
