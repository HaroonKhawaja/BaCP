\paragraph{Across sparsity.}
The margin widened at every step of the schedule. Mean $\Delta$ rose
\result{summary.delta.sparsity.0.95}, \result{summary.delta.sparsity.0.97},
\result{summary.delta.sparsity.0.99} and
\result{summary.delta.sparsity.0.999} at 0.95, 0.97, 0.99 and 0.999 --
strictly monotone, over twelve cells per column spanning four architectures
and three criteria. At 0.95 both arms sit near the dense reference and there
is little damage to repair. By 0.999 the baseline has lost the network:
ResNet-50 under WANDA falls to \result{resnet50.ip.wanda.0.999}, barely above
chance on ten classes, and the contrastive objective returns
\result{resnet50.delta.wanda.0.999}. That monotonicity is the paper's central
result, and it is the shape a representation-level effect predicts. The mean
over the whole grid, \result{summary.delta.mean}, is the wrong summary of it:
that figure is dominated by the 0.999 column and conceals the trend that makes
the mechanism legible.

\paragraph{Across criteria.}
No block reverses in aggregate: mean $\Delta$ is
\result{summary.delta.criterion.magnitude} under magnitude,
\result{summary.delta.criterion.snip} under SNIP-it and
\result{summary.delta.criterion.wanda} under WANDA. The three share everything
but the score, so this is the criterion-agnosticism evidence. The ordering is
not an independent finding: WANDA leads because it damages the model most and
therefore leaves the most to recover, which is the sparsity trend read along a
second axis. Sign consistency is not universal --
\result{summary.delta.npositive} of \result{summary.delta.ncells} pairs are
positive -- and the negatives are not scattered but concentrated in a single
model, which the next paragraph takes up rather than averaging away.

\paragraph{Where it does not help.}
VGG-11 is negative on average, \result{summary.delta.model.vgg11}, against
\result{summary.delta.model.resnet50} for ResNet-50,
\result{summary.delta.model.resnet34} for ResNet-34 and
\result{summary.delta.model.vgg19} for VGG-19. Its own baseline explains why:
I.P. at 0.95 reaches \result{vgg11.ip.magnitude.0.95} against a dense
\result{vgg11.dense.none.0.0}, so gradual pruning costs it almost nothing even
at 95\% sparsity, and there is no representation damage to repair. Roughly
85\% of VGG-11's parameters sit in a classifier MLP that is largely redundant
for $32\times32$ inputs. A contrastive term added to an objective already at
its ceiling is a competing gradient, and it costs a few tenths. We report this
as the boundary condition of the claim rather than as an anomaly: the
mechanism that yields \result{summary.delta.sparsity.0.999} where pruning
destroys the network predicts approximately nothing where pruning does no
harm, and that is what we measured.

\paragraph{A fourth architecture.}
The three families above are all dense-convolutional. To test whether the
effect is specific to that shape we added MobileNetV2
\citep{sandler2018mobilenetv2}, which is inverted-residual and entirely
depthwise-separable, with \num{2.2}M prunable weights against ResNet-34's
\num{21}M. Three seeds, all three criteria, both arms at SGD $0.1$
(Table~\ref{tab:mobilenet}). Every criterion traces the same shape: a few
points while the network is only lightly damaged, a large margin at the
sparsity where the baseline begins to fail, and nothing once both arms are
dead. What differs between criteria is \emph{where} that failure falls.
WANDA breaks first --- both arms are at chance by 0.99. Magnitude survives to
0.995, where I.P.\ becomes unstable at
\resultpm{mobilenet.ip.magnitude.0.995} (one seed already at chance) while
BaCP holds \resultpm{mobilenet.bacp.magnitude.0.995}, a margin of
\result{mobilenet.delta.magnitude.0.995}. SNIP-it survives furthest, still
returning \result{mobilenet.bacp.snip.0.995} against
\result{mobilenet.ip.snip.0.995} at the same sparsity. So on this
architecture the criterion sets the sparsity at which the network breaks, and
the contrastive objective is worth most just below that point --- which is
the damage-based reading of Table~\ref{tab:main} stated in a setting where
sparsity and damage can be varied separately.

Two caveats belong with these numbers. WANDA's statistic is undefined for
grouped convolutions in our implementation --- \verb|F.unfold| is
group-unaware --- so those layers fall back to magnitude ranking. On
MobileNetV2 that is \result{mobilenet.wanda.dwlayers} of
\result{mobilenet.wanda.convlayers} convolutional layers, holding
\result{mobilenet.wanda.fallback.pct}\% of the prunable weights; the
remaining \result{mobilenet.wanda.scored.pct}\% is scored as WANDA proper.
The row is therefore not purely WANDA and we do not present it as such. And
the WANDA cell at 0.99 is a collapse rather than a result: its per-seed
accuracies are \num{39.76}, \num{10.00} and \num{10.00} against BaCP's three
dead seeds, so the baseline mean of \result{mobilenet.ip.wanda.0.99} is one
surviving run averaged with two failures. We register no $\Delta$ there.

\paragraph{A second dataset.}
Everything above is CIFAR-10. We repeated the grid on CIFAR-100 with
ResNet-34 --- the one backbone with no published comparator row, so these
numbers carry no reproduction pressure --- under the same matched protocol,
three criteria, four sparsities, three seeds (Table~\ref{tab:cifar100}). The
protocol was checked rather than assumed to transfer: a 100-way
cross-entropy has a different loss scale than a 10-way one, so a
learning-rate probe was run first, and the CIFAR-10 setting won by
\num{6.82} points; nothing was retuned. BaCP is ahead in \emph{all twelve
cells}, and by a larger margin than on CIFAR-10 in eleven of them. The
ordering is the one Table~\ref{tab:main} shows: small margins while the
network is intact, large ones where the baseline begins to fail ---
\result{c100.delta.wanda.0.99} for WANDA at 0.99, where I.P.\ falls to
\result{c100.ip.wanda.0.99} while BaCP holds
\resultpm{c100.bacp.wanda.0.99}, and \result{c100.delta.magnitude.0.999} for
magnitude at 0.999. The single cell where CIFAR-10 shows the larger gain is
WANDA at 0.999, where both arms are near-destroyed --- I.P.\ scores
\result{c100.ip.wanda.0.999} against a chance floor of \num{1.00} --- and
there is correspondingly little left to recover.

Two things temper this. The dense reference is
\resultpm{c100.dense}, some five points below what this backbone reaches on
CIFAR-100 under a full-length schedule; the second dataset inherits the
budget limitation of Section~\ref{sec:method} rather than answering it. And
its seed spread is wider than CIFAR-10's, so the sub-two-point margins in the
magnitude column at 0.95--0.99 are modest relative to run-to-run noise, while
the large cells carry the result. What does not vary is the variance itself:
BaCP's standard deviation is below I.P.'s in all twelve cells, frequently by
an order of magnitude (\resultpm{c100.bacp.snip.0.99} against
\resultpm{c100.ip.snip.0.99}), the same pattern seen on every architecture in
Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\paragraph{Which contrastive objective.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} changes two keys: the contrastive formulation and the
projection-head mode (\S\ref{sec:cap}). \emph{Legacy} is the stacked
$2B \times 2B$ form with per-model trainable heads; \emph{control} is CAP's
\citep{xu2022cap} rectangular $B \times N$ functional with one frozen shared
head. Legacy led control at every measured sparsity --- why it is the objective
studied, and why the losing arm is reported --- and both contrastive arms sat at
or above I.P.: the direction does not depend on the realisation, only its size.
The legacy margin held near
\result{resnet50.abldelta.magnitude.0.95} through
\result{resnet50.abldelta.magnitude.0.97} points before rising to
\result{resnet50.abldelta.magnitude.0.99} at 0.99 --- so the grid was extended
to 0.999. Caveats: single seed; a gap above but not far above the
\result{cifar10.noise.floor.lo}--\result{cifar10.noise.floor.hi}-point noise
floor; an earlier protocol, so ordering only; and a winner carrying the
trainable head (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). That ordering is not what our
account of the mechanism predicts, and we have none for it.
