\section{Ethics statement}
\label{sec:stmt:ethics}

This research involves no human subjects, no crowdsourced labour and no
personal, private or identifiable data. No study was conducted with
participants, so no institutional review or equivalent approval was required or
sought. No data was collected, scraped, annotated or curated for this paper.

All experiments use CIFAR-10, an existing public image-classification
benchmark, obtained through the standard library loader and used unmodified for
its intended purpose. We note for completeness that CIFAR-10 was labelled from a
larger collection of images gathered from the web, so we make no claim that its
contents were curated for consent or for representational balance; we use it
because it is the benchmark under which the comparators we quote were published,
not because we regard it as an unproblematic sample.

The submission releases no dataset, no model weights and no checkpoints, and the
models involved --- small convolutional image classifiers --- are neither
generative nor dual-use. We have read the NeurIPS Code of Ethics and believe the
work conforms to it. We report the limitations of the work, the variants that
performed worse, and the earlier runs that were discarded, rather than only the
results that favour our claim.
